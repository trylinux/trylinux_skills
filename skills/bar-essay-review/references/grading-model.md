# How NCBE written grading works (background)

Use this to explain scores to the user. It does not change the grading procedure.

## Who grades
- Each jurisdiction grades the MEE and MPT. NCBE writes the grading materials (MEE analyses and MPT drafters' point sheets) and trains graders at a workshop held right after each exam.
- At the workshop, graders silently read real examinee answers for each item, grade them, and discuss outliers. Grading materials can be refined or reweighted afterward.

## Scale
- NCBE grades on a 1-6 scale at its workshop because it is memorable and avoids bunching in the middle.
- Jurisdictions reserve 0 for a blank page or a completely nonresponsive answer. Any honest attempt earns at least a 1. Zeros are left out of the group used to scale essay scores to the MBE.
- Jurisdictions may use other raw scales. A third-party prep site (Smart Bar Prep) reports, for example: 0-6 (Alabama, Illinois, New Jersey, Texas, Vermont), 1-6 (Arizona, Arkansas, Colorado), 0-7 (Massachusetts), 0-10 (New York), 0-20 (Pennsylvania), 1-5 (Hawaii), and 1-6 per question with 0 for non-answers (Tennessee). Kentucky is not on that list. Verify with the jurisdiction before relying on any of these.

## Relative grading
- Graders rank-order papers: the best in the pile get the top score even if imperfect, and the weakest get the bottom score even if they contain some content. Absolute grading is acceptable only if it is consistent and still spreads scores.
- Graders should use the full scale. Expect fewer 1s and 6s and more 3s and 4s, but every point should appear. If most papers get the same score, the item contributes little to separating examinees.

## Calibration
- Current NCBE guidance (Olson, 2019): review a calibration packet of at least 30 papers before grading, and pick a benchmark paper for each score point. With multiple graders, compare grades about every five papers and resolve differences. Earlier NCBE guidance (Gundersen, 2015) described repeated rounds with sets of about 10 papers until graders agree.
- To catch drift from fatigue or streaks of strong or weak papers, embed calibration papers with hidden scores in each grader's pile and revisit the first 10-15 papers graded.
- Grade in a compressed period to keep standards stable.

## What graders weigh
- Responding to the call, stating the rule, marshaling the relevant facts, and following format and tone instructions.
- Partial credit for correct rules and sound reasoning even when the conclusion is wrong or the doctrine is misnamed.
- Only what is on the page. No credit for analysis the examinee did not reach.
- Writing quality counts, especially on the MPT. Typos generally do not. UBE jurisdictions must consider writing skill but assign it no fixed weight.

## Scaling and weights
- Raw written scores are scaled to the jurisdiction's MBE mean and standard deviation. Scaling preserves rank order, so what matters is how a paper compares with other papers on the same exam. It also corrects for harder or easier questions and stronger or weaker cohorts (February cohorts usually score lower than July).
- UBE weights: MBE 50%, MEE 30%, MPT 20%.
- Some non-UBE jurisdictions weight or scale differently. For example, Smart Bar Prep reports that South Dakota does not scale its written scores to the MBE and instead sets a minimum passing score for each component.

## Development
- Items and grading materials go through roughly two to three years of drafting, outside expert review, and pretesting with newly licensed lawyers before use.
