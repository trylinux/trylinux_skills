# Grading the MPT

Read this before grading a Multistate Performance Test answer.

## Format
- Two 90-minute tasks per administration. Each item has a **File** (the facts: a task memo from a supervising attorney plus documents such as transcripts, file memos, wills, deeds, policies, or pleadings) and a **Library** (the law: cases, statutes, rules, treatise excerpts), set in the fictional jurisdiction of Franklin.
- The MPT tests lawyering skills, not memorized law. It is a closed universe. Everything needed is in the File and Library.
- From July 2026 through February 2028, Family Law and Trusts and Estates are tested regularly through the MPT. Expect statutory-interpretation tasks in those areas.

## Anatomy of an NCBE drafters' point sheet
1. **Disclaimer:** the point sheet lists the issues a thorough answer might address; it is not a model answer.
2. **Task description:** who the client is, what the examinee must produce (objective memo, persuasive brief, letter, etc.), and what the File and Library contain. It may say some issues must be resolved by applying statutory text with no case-law guidance.
3. **Format and overview:** any required format. Often "no required format," but there may be instructions such as "do not write a separate statement of facts; integrate the facts into the analysis."
4. **Facts:** a chronological summary for the grader's convenience. If the task memo says not to write a statement of facts, an examinee who writes one is ignoring an instruction.
5. **Legal authorities:** a summary of the Library.
6. **Analysis:** organized to mirror the questions in the task memo, in bulleted form, ending in the conclusions the drafters expect.
7. **NOTES:** handling of ambiguities in the call and alternative readings that deserve credit.

Point sheets give **no percentage weights**. Assign weights in proportion to the analysis the point sheet devotes to each question, and say what weights you used. With no point sheet, weight by the task memo: one row per question the memo asks, in proportion to how much analysis each needs, plus a 10% row for the overall recommendation if the memo asks for one. Format compliance is not a weighted row; it is handled in the writing adjustment.

## Grading checklist

### 1. Instructions and format (gatekeeping)
- Correct document type and audience (objective memo to a partner reads differently from a persuasive brief to a court or a letter to a client).
- Correct tone: objective tasks weigh both sides and predict; persuasive tasks advocate without misstating law or facts.
- Every format instruction followed (for example, no separate statement of facts, use of headings, required caption).
- Each question in the task memo answered, in the memo's order. A skipped question loses all of its weight.
- Any scoping instruction obeyed: "analyze each issue separately even if one is dispositive," "do not address X," "assume Y." Analysis outside the scope earns nothing; a skipped issue the memo said to analyze anyway loses its weight.

Format failures do not zero out good analysis, but they cost a band step if a grader would notice them immediately.

### 2. Use of the Library
- Identifies the controlling rules and walks through them in the right sequence when one rule points to another (for example, a rule that defers to exceptions in a different rule).
- Reads the cases accurately, extracts the holding and the test, and applies the reasoning, not just the result.
- Distinguishes or reconciles adverse authority.
- Where the Library gives no guidance, applies statutory text directly, including definitions sections.
- Cites Library authorities by name. Unlike the MEE, MPT graders expect the examinee to tie conclusions to the specific authorities provided.

### 3. Use of the File
- Pulls the facts that matter from all File documents, not just the task memo.
- Treats rules, regulations, policies, contracts, and ordinances that appear in the File as authorities. They are often where the dispositive test lives, and an answer that ignores them loses credit the same as ignoring a Library case.
- Notices what a party does and does not know. Some tasks turn on the difference between facts known to the client's lawyer and facts available to the opposing side or the court.
- Integrates facts into the analysis rather than restating them.

### 4. Outside law
No credit for law not in the Library. If outside law contradicts the Library, the answer is wrong on that point. Real-world citations are a sign the examinee is not working from the Library.

### 5. Stronger vs. weaker answers
Point sheets sometimes describe both:
- **Stronger:** reaches the right conclusion by engaging the specific provisions, definitions, and documents that control.
- **Weaker but still correct:** reaches the right conclusion without walking through the controlling text.
Score stronger as Full and weaker-but-correct as Substantial unless the point sheet says otherwise.

### 6. Close calls with no official answer
Some MPT questions are genuinely close (for example, which of two dates a marriage began, on mixed evidence). With no point sheet, accept either reasoned conclusion. Grade on how well the answer weighs the evidence on both sides; an objective memo that argues one side and ignores the adverse facts is capped at Substantial on that row.

### 7. Ambiguity in the call
If the task memo can be read two ways and the point sheet says either reading leads to the same analysis, accept either. If an examinee spots a genuine ambiguity in a statute and argues it, award at least Substantial credit even if the point sheet prefers the other reading.

## Reading extracted text
Item text extracted from a PDF may carry watermark fragments ("Do Not Copy" split into letters), page numbers, and table-of-contents dot leaders. Read around them. Never treat them as part of the File or Library.

## Errors in the point sheet
Point sheets are long and occasionally contain a stray sentence that does not match the task (for example, a description of claims that appear nowhere in the File). If the point sheet contradicts the task memo or the File, follow the memo and the File, and tell the user about the inconsistency.
